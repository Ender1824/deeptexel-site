DeepTexel for Unreal Engine 5

Textures that keep their detail as the camera gets close. Where the view magnifies a texture beyond its real resolution, DeepTexel builds new, finer detail on the GPU from the texture's own finer regions (fractal self-similarity), up to 1,024 times its real resolution, only where you look, and blends it in seamlessly.

No AI model, no extra art, no network: each texture needs one small detail pack (about 2 MB), generated in the editor with a right-click in under a second.

Requirements

  • Unreal Engine 5.8, Windows (Win64).
  • DirectX 12 with Shader Model 6 (the default for new UE 5.8 projects). Any DX12 GPU that supports SM6 works; tested on NVIDIA (RTX 5080) and AMD.
  • Project setting Engine > Physics > Support UV From Hit Results must be on (restart the editor after changing it). The component tells you in its Status and the log if it is off.
  • For very close views (beyond about 6x), a small near clip plane (Project Settings > Engine > General Settings > Near Clip Plane, e.g. 0.05). The default 10 cm clips away surfaces you are closer than 10 cm to. The sample viewer sets it for you.

Quick start: the sample map

  1. Enable Support UV From Hit Results (above) and restart the editor.
  2. Open Plugins > DeepTexel Content > Samples > FractalDetail_Sample and press Play. (If the Content Browser shows no Plugins folder: its Settings > Show Plugin Content.)
  3. Fly with WASD + mouse (E/Q up and down), or roll the mouse wheel to move towards whatever is at the centre of the screen. Speed follows the distance to the surface, so you can glide right up to the mosaic or the stone block. Press F to switch the detail off and on to compare. The top-left shows the magnification and the detail status.

The sample shows both ways to use the plugin:

  • Mosaic: an AFractalPicture actor, a ready-made framed picture.
  • Stone block: an ordinary static mesh with a lit material (M_FractalDetail_Lit) and a FractalDetail component, the way you would add it to your own assets.

Adding DeepTexel to your own mesh

  1. Generate the texture's pack. In the Content Browser, right-click the texture > Generate DeepTexel Pack. A <Texture>_DetailPack asset appears next to it. (Several textures can be selected at once.)
  2. Use the material function. In the mesh's material, where the texture is sampled, put MF_FractalDetail instead:
  3. UV input: the mesh's UV0 (a TextureCoordinate node, index 0, no tiling).
  4. Texture input: the texture, as a Texture Object (parameter or node).
  5. Color output: linear colour. Connect it to Base Color (lit) or Emissive (unlit), or into whatever the texture fed before. Samples/Materials/M_FractalDetail_Lit is a complete example.
  6. Add the component. Add a DeepTexel component to the actor and set its Texture (the same texture) and Pack (its pack). If the actor has several meshes, the first static mesh is used; call SetTargetMesh to choose another, and set Material Index if the material is not slot 0.

That's all. Play, and move close to the mesh.

Performance and memory

Measured on an RTX 5080 at 1080p:

  • Idle: components that are not close to the camera use no GPU memory and no GPU time.
  • Active: about 0.8 GB of GPU memory per active component (search tables, work buffers, ten detail levels). At most FractalDetail.MaxActive (default 2) are active at once.
  • Building detail: about 2 ms of GPU time per frame (GPU Budget Ms), and only while a level is being built: each level takes about 0.12-0.17 s. A full close-up (9 levels) is ready in about 1-1.5 s; after that the cost is the material's blending only.
  • Pack generation: about 0.2-0.6 s per 4K texture, in the editor.
  • Slow GPUs: the plugin measures its own GPU time. On a GPU more than 20x slower than the reference (for example a small integrated GPU) it switches itself off and the material shows the plain texture.

Limitations

  • Windows, DX12 / SM6 only in this version.
  • The material's UV input must be the mesh's UV0 as-is. Tiling in the mesh's UVs is fine; scaling or offsetting UVs inside the material is not supported (the component locates detail through the mesh's UV0).
  • One texture per component (the one at the screen centre gets the detail). Use one component per mesh that should get detail.
  • Regenerate the pack if you change the texture's pixels or size (the component reports a size mismatch). Compression settings can change freely.
  • Detail is generated, not recovered: it continues the texture's own look at finer scales. It is not a replacement for real high-resolution source art where accuracy matters.

How it works

When the camera magnifies a texture beyond its real resolution, the component builds the next 2x level of a 1024 x 1024 patch around the point at the screen centre, and keeps going a level at a time as you get closer (up to 10 levels). For each small patch of the upscaled image it searches the texture's own example patches (320,000 of them, at two scales and in eight orientations, clustered into 1,024 groups by the detail pack) for the one whose surroundings match best, and copies that example's fine detail, scaled to the local contrast. Back-projection makes sure that shrinking the result gives back the original pixels exactly, so detail never changes what the texture looks like from further away. The same spot always gets the same detail.
