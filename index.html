How it works

Every texture is its own best reference

Real-world surfaces and artwork look alike across scales: a brush stroke contains smaller strokes, a rock face contains smaller cracks. DeepTexel uses that.

1

Right-click, generate

In the editor, right-click a texture and choose Generate DeepTexel Pack. In under a second the GPU clusters 320,000 example patches from the texture into a pack of about 2 MB.

2

Look closer

When the view magnifies the texture past its resolution, the component builds the next 2× level around the point you're looking at, matching each patch against the texture's own examples and copying their fine detail.

3

Keep going

Level by level, up to ten levels (1,024×), each in about 0.13 s. Back-projection keeps every level faithful to the pixels above it, so from further away the texture looks exactly as before.

No AI model

No network, no training data, no extra art. The detail comes from your texture alone, and the same spot always gets the same detail.

Within a 2 ms budget

Detail is built on the GPU through Unreal's render graph, inside a measured per-frame budget, and only while a level is being built.

Drop-in

A component and a material function. Works with your own meshes and lit or unlit materials. Ready-made framed picture actor included.

Only where you look

Components wake when the camera gets close and release their GPU memory when you move on. Idle textures cost nothing.

Safe on any GPU

It measures its own GPU time and switches itself off on GPUs too slow for it, falling back to the plain texture.

Sample included

A sample map with a fly-through viewer: glide right up to a mosaic and a stone block, and press F to compare.
